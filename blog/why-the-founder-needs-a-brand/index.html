Why the founder needs a brand of his own

Nino Mihilli· October 5, 2026 ·3 min read

I resisted this for a long time. Building a personal brand felt adjacent to self-promotion, and self-promotion felt like something people did instead of building.

I was wrong about it, and the reason is specific to how a portfolio works.

Company brands do not transfer. You do.

Each company in a portfolio has to earn its own reputation with its own customers. A staffing client does not care that we also build software. A player does not care about the holding company. I have written before that customer bases do not share across ventures, and that remains true.

But something does transfer, and it is the founder.

The person who did right by a client in one business is the same person starting the next one. When you launch venture number six, nobody knows the company — but people who have worked with you know you. That is the only asset that carries across, and in a portfolio that starts things regularly, it is the difference between beginning at zero and beginning with a hearing.

What it actually does

It shortens the trust conversation. Every new venture, partnership, and hire starts with an implicit question about whether these people are serious and whether they will do what they say. A track record that is visible answers part of that before the first meeting.

It brings you deal flow. The best opportunities I have had did not come from searching. Someone thought of me because they knew what I work on. That only happens if what you work on is legible to people outside your immediate circle.

It helps hiring. Good operators are choosy about who they work for. Someone who can see how you think about a business before they interview arrives with a much better sense of whether they want the job — which produces better fits in both directions.

It survives the companies. Businesses get sold, wound down, or changed beyond recognition. The reputation of the person who ran them persists.

What it is not

It is not audience for its own sake. Followers are not the asset. A hundred people who know exactly what you do and would take your call is worth more than a hundred thousand who recognize your name and could not say why.

It is not a separate activity from the work. The version that fails is the one where the brand is a performance disconnected from anything you actually operate. The version that works is a byproduct — you build things, you learn, you say what you learned plainly, and over time people understand what you know.

It is not the company's brand. T47 Group has to stand on its own with partners. Xsatori Labs has to earn its own reputation with clients. My name does not do that work and should not be asked to.

How I actually approach it

Write about what you are doing while you are doing it. Not lessons from a mountaintop — decisions in progress, with the reasoning visible and the uncertainty admitted.

Be specific enough to be wrong. Generic advice costs nothing to give and is worth what it costs. The useful posts are the ones that name a number, take a position, or say something that some readers will disagree with.

Say the unflattering parts. The things I have gotten wrong are more useful to other operators than the things that worked, and they are more credible, because everyone knows twenty years of building includes failures.

Publish consistently rather than brilliantly. Nobody is waiting for your essay. But over years, showing up regularly is what turns a name into a reputation.

The honest downside

It makes you visible, and visibility means criticism, including unfair criticism. It also ties your reputation to your companies in both directions — when one goes badly, that is attached to you personally in a way it would not be otherwise.

I think that trade is worth making, partly because the accountability is real. Knowing that what I said publicly will be measured against what the businesses actually do is a useful constraint.

That is what ninomihilli.com is for, and it is why I finally stopped resisting it. Not because the world needed another founder writing posts, but because in a portfolio that expects to keep starting things, the person is the only thing that carries forward.
