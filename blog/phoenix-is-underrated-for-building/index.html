Phoenix is an underrated place to build a company

Nino Mihilli· September 21, 2026 ·3 min read

Every city claims to be an emerging tech hub. Most of the claims are marketing. I want to give the honest version for Phoenix, including what is genuinely harder here.

What is real

Cost structure that changes what you can attempt. Not just salaries — office space, warehouse space, the cost of living that determines what someone needs to earn. A team here has a longer runway on the same money than a team in the Bay Area or New York. That difference is not a rounding error; it decides how many attempts you get.

A real labor market across the whole stack. This is what people miss. Phoenix has software talent, and it also has warehouse, logistics, automotive, construction, and healthcare workforces at scale. If your business touches physical operations — and plenty of good businesses do — you can staff it here. Running Flatstaffing has made this concrete for me in a way it would not be otherwise.

Businesses that will actually take your call. A midsize company here will meet a local vendor. That is much less true in denser markets where everyone is already saturated with pitches. For a B2B business, the accessibility of the local market is an underrated advantage.

Growth that keeps arriving. People and companies keep moving here. That means a market that expands under you, and a steady inflow of experienced operators from more expensive cities.

Fewer distractions. There is less ambient noise about funding rounds and less pressure to perform a certain kind of startup. That sounds soft. In practice it means teams here tend to focus on customers, because there is no alternative scoreboard.

What is genuinely harder

Capital density. There is money here and it is growing, but there is less of it and it is more conservative than in the major hubs. If your business needs a large early round from investors who see dozens of companies like yours a month, that ecosystem is thinner. Founders here raise out of state more often than not, and that is a real friction.

Depth in narrow specialties. Generalist engineering talent is available. Deeply specialized talent in a narrow field is harder to find locally, and you will compete for it with remote offers from companies paying coastal rates. Remote work has helped and it has also meant your local hiring pool is now everyone's hiring pool.

I would add a third thing that is not a business disadvantage but is real: the summer is genuinely difficult, and it affects recruiting from cooler places more than people admit.

Who Phoenix is right for

Businesses with a physical component. Logistics, staffing, distribution, field services, anything with a facility. The cost structure and labor market are genuinely strong.

Bootstrapped or profitably-funded companies. If you are funding from revenue, the lower cost base extends your runway substantially, and the thinner venture ecosystem stops being a disadvantage because you were not going to use it.

B2B companies selling to midsize businesses. The local market is accessible and growing.

Second-time founders. Cheaper to try things, less pressure to perform, easier to be quietly patient.

Who it is probably wrong for

If you need a large venture round from investors who require regular in-person contact, and your business has a genuine land-grab dynamic where speed beats capital efficiency, the hubs still have advantages that honesty requires acknowledging. You can build that company here. You will do more traveling.

What I would tell someone considering it

The advantage is not that Phoenix is secretly a tech hub. It is that Phoenix lets you build a durable company on less money, in a market that is growing, with access to both software and physical labor markets.

That is a specific set of conditions. It happens to be exactly the set of conditions that suits how I build — cash-flow first, keep what you build, do not sell it in three years. Someone optimizing for a fast fundraise and a fast exit would weigh it differently, and they would not be wrong.

We run T47 Group out of Phoenix and none of our companies would be cheaper or easier to run somewhere else. That has been true for twenty years and it is more true now than when I started.
